## Results

Oxford Nanopore long-read sequencing was employed in six bacteriophage genome studies to resolve complete or near-complete genomic assemblies. Genome sizes ranged from 38.7 kb (VB_ST_SPNIS2) to 98.6 kb, with circular topologies predominating (five studies). Four studies utilized nanopore as a component of hybrid sequencing strategies combined with short-read Illumina data, while two studies employed nanopore alone (Fyn8 and 2019SD1). Flye was the most common primary assembly algorithm (three studies), with Canu used in two studies. Medaka polishing was applied in three studies; two studies did not perform polishing. Phage hosts spanned multiple bacterial genera including *Salmonella*, *Pseudomonas*, *Shigella*, and *Wolbachia*, plus the insect-associated endosymbiont *Wolbachia*. Five studies achieved complete genome sequences; one resulted in a draft assembly. Notable findings included discovery of novel polyvalent phages with broad host ranges, identification of temperate phages encoding defense mechanisms, and demonstration of microencapsulated phage formulations as antimicrobial agents against multidrug-resistant pathogens. The capacity of nanopore sequencing to generate long reads proved instrumental in resolving repetitive regions and achieving chromosomal completeness across diverse phage types.

## Methods

A systematic literature search was conducted across PubMed for studies reporting bacteriophage genome assembly incorporating Oxford Nanopore sequencing technology. Inclusion criteria required: (I1) use of Oxford Nanopore long-read sequencing; (I2) primary research on isolated bacteriophage genomes; (I3) reported complete or draft genome sequence assembly; and (I4) peer-reviewed publication. The initial search identified 172 studies. Following duplicate removal, 170 studies underwent abstract screening, with 145 excluded based on title/abstract review. Twenty-one studies marked as uncertain underwent full-text review, yielding one additional inclusion. This resulted in a total of six included studies reporting phage genome sequences assembled with Oxford Nanopore technology. Data extraction captured phage identification, host organism, genome size, topology, ONT platform (MinION/GridION), assembly and polishing tools, hybrid sequencing approaches, and completeness status. PRISMA guidelines were followed throughout the screening and data extraction process.
